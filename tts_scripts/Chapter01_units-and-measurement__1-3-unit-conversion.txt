Unit Conversion.

It is often necessary to convert from one unit to another (as you've already seen). If you are reading a cookbook, some quantities may be expressed in milliliters and you need to convert them to cups.

Suppose we want to convert 80 meters to kilometers.

First, start with the input value you want to convert as a fraction (i.e., itself over one).

Second, determine the conversion factor necessary. A conversion factor is a ratio that describes how much of one unit is equal to another.

Third, multiply by the ratio so that unit from the input value cancels (i.e., unit/unit equals 1 ).

Using these steps, we have:

80 meters times one kilometer per ten to the third meters equals 0.08 kilometers.

Note that the unwanted m cancels, leaving only the desired km unit.

Important guidance for this course.

On exams, you will be provided with a unit lookup table, not Python code.

A good workflow is:

First, perform the unit conversion analytically.

Second, use Python (and Sigh-pie constants) to confirm the numerical result.

This approach reinforces both physical reasoning and computational fluency, which will be important throughout the course.

Example Problem: Converting iron's density.

The Problem.
The density of iron is 7.86 grams per cubic centimeter under standard conditions. Convert this to kilograms per cubic meter.

The worked solution follows.

The Model.

Density is a scalar quantity defined as mass per unit volume. To convert between unit systems, we multiply by conversion factors equal to one, chosen so that unwanted units cancel and desired units remain.

In this problem, we must:

Convert grams to kilograms, and

Convert cubic centimeters to cubic meters.

Because volume scales with the cube of length, the length conversion factor must be applied three times.

The Math.

The required conversion factors are

One kilogram equals ten to the third grams, and one centimeter equals ten to the minus second meters.

Starting from the given density, 7.86 grams per cubic centimeter, we apply the conversion factors:

7.86 grams per cubic centimeter
equals 7.86 grams per cubic centimeter times one kilogram per ten to the third grams, times one centimeter per ten to the minus second meters, cubed,
equals 7.86 divided by ten to the third times ten to the minus sixth kilograms per cubic meter,
which equals 7.86 times ten to the third kilograms per cubic meter.

The Conclusion.

The density of iron expressed in S I units is

7.86 grams per cubic centimeter equals 7.86 times ten to the third kilograms per cubic meter.
